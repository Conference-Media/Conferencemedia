How it works.

  1. Planning call. We learn what the content is for (tickets, sponsors, speakers) and build a shot list, interview plan and delivery schedule with you.
  2. Venue and AV prep. We coordinate with your venue and AV provider ahead of time, so audio is clean and we know where to set up.

What the video does after the event.

Good conference video keeps working long after the room empties. We shoot with three jobs in mind:

Selling next year's tickets. A recap that shows a full room, energy on stage and attendees saying why they came does more for registrations than any brochure.

Renewing sponsors. Sponsors want proof their investment was seen. Booth footage, activation clips and sponsor interviews give your partnerships team something concrete to send before renewal.

Attracting speakers. Speakers say yes to events that make them look good. A clean keynote recording and a short clip they can post on LinkedIn are part of the pitch for next year.

Choosing a conference videographer.

Whoever you hire, these questions separate a conference specialist from a general video crew:

  • Can I see full conference work? Ask for recaps and keynote recordings from real conferences, not weddings or brand ads.
  • When will I get each deliverable? Get the turnaround for photos, the recap and full sessions in writing.
  • How will you capture stage audio? The answer should involve the venue's AV team, not a camera microphone at the back of the room.
  • Who covers the sponsors? Sponsor footage is easy to forget on a busy day and hard to recreate later.
  • How will the files be organized? Galleries sorted by session, sponsor and speaker get used. A folder of 3,000 unsorted files doesn't.
